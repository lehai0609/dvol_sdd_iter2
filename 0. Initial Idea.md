# 1) Target (what you predict)

**Daily ΔDVOL** for BTC (or ETH):

yt≡Close(DVOL)t−Close(DVOL)t−1y_t \equiv \text{Close}(DVOL)_t-\text{Close}(DVOL)_{t-1}

Why DVOL? It’s a 30-day _implied_ volatility index published by Deribit; it’s the cleanest, tradeable proxy for “market IV”. CDD exposes a **daily DVOL OHLC** endpoint, so you don’t need to stitch raw option chains. ([Crypto Data Download](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/api/ "API Solutions"))

# 3) Label & feature engineering (exact recipe)

All **features at t** must be computable before New York close (pick a fixed cut-off; keep it consistent).

## Label (regression)

- **y_t = ΔDVOL_t = DVOL_close_t − DVOL_close_{t−1}** (BTC or ETH).
    

## Direction label (optional classification head)

- **up/flat/down** with a dead-zone (e.g., +σ/−σ band around 0 using in-sample σ of ΔDVOL).
    

## Features (daily)

Think “low leakage, interpretable, small set.” Here’s a deployable minimal set:

**Price / realized-vol / jumps**

- RV_1, RV_5, RV_22: close-to-close realized vol (annualize).
    
- HAR terms: RV_t−1, mean(RV_{t−2…t−5}), mean(RV_{t−6…t−22}). (HAR often strong in crypto.) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0275531921001616?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Forecasting volatility of Bitcoin"))
    
- **Overnight gap**: |close_{t−1} − open_t| / close_{t−1}.
    
- **Jump proxy**: Realized Variance − Bipower Variation (clip at 0); flags large discontinuities (IV slope reacts to jump direction). ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167637724000713?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Implied volatility slopes and jumps in bitcoin options market"), [SpringerLink](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s40854-024-00653-z.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Price dynamics and volatility jumps in bitcoin options - Springer"))
    

**Options / flow**

- DVOL level_t−1 (yesterday’s close)—captures mean-reversion.
    
- **Term slope**: DVOL_high − DVOL_low (proxy for intraday dispersion); or if you store 7d vs 30d DVOL when available, use slope(7d→30d). (Start with OHLC spread from DVOL endpoint.) ([Crypto Data Download](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/api/ "API Solutions"))
    
- **Net Vega_t** (from CDD summary) and its 5-day z-score.
    
- **Avg IV_t** (CDD summary) − DVOL_{t−1}: a “rich/cheap” proxy. ([Crypto Data Download](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/api/ "API Solutions"))
    
- **Volume & OI proxies**: from options summary—USD Volume_t, Open Interest (if present), 5-day changes. ([Crypto Data Download](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/api/ "API Solutions"))
    

**Funding / basis (risk appetite)**

- **Perp funding (8h & 1h)** aggregated to daily mean and standard deviation; 5-day change. (Risk-on/off flavor.) ([Crypto Data Download](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/api/ "API Solutions"))
    

**On-chain (macro microstructure)**

- **Tx count, fees, mempool congestion proxies** from CDD BTC chain summary (level and 5-day change). ([Crypto Data Download](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/api/ "API Solutions"))
    

> Notes  
> • Keep feature count modest (≤25) to reduce overfit.  
> • Standardize (RobustScaler) using train-only stats.  
> • All features lagged so they’re known before the DVOL close you’re predicting.

# 5) Modeling (baseline → LSTM → (optional) Transformer)

## Baselines (must beat these)

- **Lasso / Ridge** on the feature table.
    
- **LightGBM** with 250 trees, max_depth 4–6. (Great sanity check.)
    

## LSTM (production-friendly spec)

- **Input**: rolling window of **90 days** (sequence length L=90), feature dim ~15–25.
    
- **Architecture**:
    
    - LSTM(64) → Dropout(0.1) → Dense(32, ReLU) → Dense(1) for ΔDVOL.
        
    - Optional branch: shared LSTM → Dense(3, softmax) for direction.
        
- **Loss**: Huber (regression) + 0.2× Cross-Entropy (classification).
    
- **Optimizer**: Adam(lr=1e-3), ReduceLROnPlateau, early stop on 20-day patience.
    
- **Regularization**: Dropout 0.1, L2=1e-5 on Dense layers.
    
- **Targets**: scale ΔDVOL by its train σ (stabilizes training).  
    Evidence that ML/LSTM helps for crypto vol forecasting: ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1042443124001306?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Forecasting Bitcoin volatility using machine learning techniques"), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04727?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Forecasting Volatility with Machine Learning and Rough"))
    

## Walk-forward training & evaluation (crucial)

- **Expanding window**: train on [start…t−30], validate on [t−29…t−1], predict **t**.
    
- Roll daily; log RMSE, MAE, **directional hit-rate** and **Spearman sign correlation**.
    
- Keep a **baseline scoreboard** to ensure your LSTM is genuinely adding value.

# 6) Map forecast → trades (two clean paths)

## A) DVOL futures (simplest, “pure IV”)

- Signal: y^t\hat{y}_t (predicted ΔDVOL).
    
- Threshold τ: choose so **expected edge > fees/slippage** (tune on train).
    
- Rule:
    
    - If y^t≥+τ\hat{y}_t \ge +\tau: **Long 1 DVOL future**
        
    - If y^t≤−τ\hat{y}_t \le -\tau: **Short 1 DVOL future**
        
    - Else: flat
        
- Roll / expiry: DVOL futures cash-settle in **USDC** on a **60-min DVOL TWAP**; size small early. ([Deribit Support](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944688376477-DVOL-Futures?utm_source=chatgpt.com "DVOL Futures – Deribit Support"))
    

## B) Options (if you prefer)

- **Delta-hedged ATM straddle** (7–21d expiry) for long-vol view; **short strangle/iron condor** for short-vol (risk-defined).
    
- Re-hedge delta once per day (EOD) to keep the P&L primarily IV-sensitive.
    
- This aligns with literature on delta-hedged “vol-premium” strategies. ([SpringerLink](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s40854-024-00653-z.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Price dynamics and volatility jumps in bitcoin options - Springer"))
    

**Risk controls (non-negotiable)**

- Max position: e.g., ≤ 2 contracts DVOL; or options: cap net vega.
    
- Event guardrails: if funding std↑ or jump proxy fires, reduce size.
    
- Daily stop: e.g., −1.5× your forecast edge or backtest 95% VaR.

# 7) Backtest math (so you can verify)

## DVOL futures P&L (1 contract)

Let **F_t** = DVOL future price (track nearest with sufficient liquidity; or a synthetic DVOL close if you prototype).

P&Lt+1=positiont⋅(Ft+1−Ft)−fees−slippage\text{P\&L}_{t+1} = \text{position}_t \cdot (F_{t+1}-F_t) - \text{fees} - \text{slippage}

At expiry, **settle on the published TWAP of DVOL** per Deribit spec. ([Deribit Support](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944688376477-DVOL-Futures?utm_source=chatgpt.com "DVOL Futures – Deribit Support"))

## Options P&L (delta-hedged)

P&Lt+1≈ΔV_option−Δhedge−theta−fees\text{P\&L}_{t+1} \approx \Delta V\_\text{option} - \Delta\text{hedge} - \text{theta} - \text{fees}

Compute via mid-marks; hedge once per day at close; include bid-ask haircuts.

Report **Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, turnover, tail loss on jump days**.

# 11) Calibration & thresholds (how to choose τ and size)

- **τ (entry)**: search over the 60–80th percentile of |ŷ| from train; pick the smallest τ whose **net** Sharpe > 0.8 (or your hurdle) in validation.
    
- **Position size**: proportional to |ŷ| but clipped by max vega (or 1–2 DVOL contracts).
    
- **Regime filter**: If jump proxy fired yesterday **and** funding std is in top decile, trade **half size** (empirically helps). Back this with a simple grid search.
